Composition

Combining monitors and protocols: concurrent(), sequential(), threshold() and observe_only().
WarningOutline

This page is an outline: a summary and the sections planned for it, not yet the documentation, apart from Sequential and Threshold. See Getting Started for a worked example.

Overview

How several monitors and protocols combine into one sentinel. Covers what a list or mapping passed to Task(sentinel=...) resolves to, the shipped protocols that combine and threshold, how decisions are ranked, instance names and paths, and nesting. resample() is planned.

Planned Sections

  • What Task(sentinel=...) resolves to: a lone protocol, or a list or mapping with at least one protocol; monitors alone are an error naming observe_only()
  • Instance names, mapping keys and paths (llm/suspicion)
  • concurrent(): every child runs; the strongest decision wins (terminate > reject > modify > escalate > proceed). A modify beside a proceed or an escalate is honoured, and the protocols that proceeded do not check the rewritten call, as for a lone modifier; two modifys conflict, so the layer rejects the call, naming the modifiers
  • observe_only(): recording scores with nothing acting on them, and collecting a benign distribution; the only way to run monitors with no protocol
  • resample() (planned)
  • Nesting compositions
  • Writing your own composition over the runner

Sequential

sequential() runs its children one at a time, in order. The first child that decides anything but escalate decides for the chain, and the children after it do not run. Use it for a cheap rule ahead of an expensive judge, or a person at the end, a human(), who is asked only what the rules could not settle.

from inspect_sentinel import human, sequential

sentinel = sequential([triage(), human(stages=["tool_call"])])

Here triage() rejects what is clearly bad, escalates what it is unsure about, and lets the rest proceed; the person is asked only about the escalated calls. The full task is examples/escalate_to_human.py.

  • Escalations are handed forward. A child that returns Decision.escalate(...) passes the step to the next child. A protocol receives the escalation in its third parameter, escalations, together with every earlier one in the chain; a monitor never receives them, so what a monitor reports does not depend on what other monitors or protocols decided. The list starts empty each time the chain runs, so a nested sequential does not see the escalations of the chain around it, and a protocol anywhere else is handed none.
  • Abstaining passes the step on. A child that returns None has not decided, so the next child runs. A rule ahead of a human() should return Decision.proceed() for the calls it is content with, or the person is asked about them too.
  • Monitors fall through. A monitor in the chain is recorded and the next child runs, since an observation is not a decision.
  • Children watch their own stages. A child that does not watch the current stage is skipped, as everywhere else.
  • When nobody decides. If every child that decided escalated, the chain returns the last escalate, passing it up to the layer above. If only monitors reported it returns proceed, and if no child took part it abstains. An escalate that reaches the top of the sentinel ends the sample as an unhandled escalation, so end a chain that can escalate with human() or with handle_escalation("terminate") or handle_escalation("proceed"); see Unhandled Escalations.
  • Final decisions. A child that calls decide_final() ends the step as usual; the chain and every layer above it are recorded as bypassed.

Each child is recorded under its instance name below the chain’s path: attempt/network_rule for a chain configured under the name attempt. In YAML the children go under children::

sentinel:
  attempt:
    name: sequential
    children:
      network_rule: {name: network_rule}
      judge: {name: llm_judge, params: {model: openai/gpt-4o-mini}}

Threshold

threshold() turns monitors’ suspicion scores into a decision. Each of escalate_at, reject_at and terminate_at is a floor: at or above it the step gets at least that action. The decision is the strongest action any score reaches, terminate over reject over escalate, and proceed when none is reached. Give at least one; to record scores with nothing acting on them, use observe_only().

A configuration that never rejects, since a rejection tells the agent no and it tries again, escalates what is suspicious to a person and ends the sample above a higher score:

from inspect_sentinel import human, sequential, threshold

sentinel = sequential(
    [
        threshold([proposed_action()], escalate_at=0.6, terminate_at=0.9),
        human(stages=["tool_call"]),
    ]
)
  • Thresholds are floors. With escalate_at=0.6, terminate_at=0.9, a score of 0.7 escalates and 0.95 terminates. Across monitors the strongest action reached wins, so one monitor at 0.95 terminates whatever the others say.
  • One number, or one per monitor and dimension. A mapping is keyed by "<monitor>.<dimension>", a bare "<dimension>" for that dimension from every monitor, a monitor’s instance name for its single-number score, or "*" for anything unlisted, the most specific key winning: escalate_at={"audit.sabotage": 0.6, "*": 0.8}. A score no key covers never triggers. A key containing . always names a monitor and one of its dimensions, never a bare dimension.
  • A threshold a stronger one always overrides is an error. escalate_at=0.9 beside terminate_at=0.8 would never escalate, so threshold() raises when it is configured, naming both. A key that one mapping lists and another does not is not such a conflict.
  • Stages. threshold() runs at each stage its monitors watch, before and after tool calls, so a proposed-action monitor and a tool-result monitor combine in one, each thresholded at its own stage. A reject is legal only before a tool call, so with reject_at every monitor must watch only BeforeToolCall. When the monitors watch both stages, ask the person at both: human(stages=["tool_call", "tool_result"]). A monitor that watches AfterGenerate judges the whole assistant turn before any of its calls runs; ask the person there with human(stages=["model_output"]), which shows the turn’s text and every call.
  • An escalation needs something after it. With escalate_at, put threshold() first in a sequential() and a human() or handle_escalation("terminate" | "proceed") after it: its other decisions end the chain, so the person is asked only when it escalates. When no monitor reported at a step, as when a bash()-only monitor skips a submit() call, threshold() decides proceed (“no monitor reported”) rather than abstaining, so the person is not asked about it, and no other link after it runs at that step either, so a later link meant as a fallback for the calls its monitors skip, such as a second threshold(), does not run there. At a stage none of its monitors watches threshold() does not run, and the chain goes on to human(), so give human() only the stages its monitors watch. An escalation that reaches the top of the sentinel with nothing to handle it ends the sample (see Unhandled Escalations). Without escalate_at it never escalates and needs nothing after it.
  • Beside a rewrite in concurrent(), the rewrite takes effect. A threshold() that decides proceed, whether its scores are low or no monitor reported, still votes, but a proceed does not contest a modify, so concurrent({"gate": threshold(...), "rewrite": rewriter()}) rewrites the call whenever the threshold proceeds. The threshold’s monitors judged the call as the agent made it and do not see the rewrite.
  • What the person sees. The explanation names the highest score that reached the threshold with its monitor’s reasoning: suspicion 0.72 from proposed_action: reads ~/.ssh. The decision also carries every reporting monitor’s references and, in metadata["observations"], each monitor’s score and explanation, so a human() after it receives every score in the escalations it is handed. inspect_ai’s approval card shows only each escalation’s explanation for now; it shows every score once its renderer change lands (see the deferred item). The agent sees none of it.
  • A failed monitor fails the step. threshold() does not decide without one of its monitors, so a monitor that raises fails the sample, with the failure in the transcript.
